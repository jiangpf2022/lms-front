<LanguagePanel language="en" label="English">

Welcome—open a blank document, but do not start with the introduction. A strong modeling paper is not a diary of what the team tried. It is an argument reconstructed in the order a reader needs: question, assumptions, model, solution, evidence, decision, and limits.

The central discipline is traceability. Every symbol should refer to a defined quantity; every equation should implement a stated mechanism; every figure should answer a question; every conclusion should trace to evidence. When this chain is intact, complex mathematics becomes readable. When it is broken, polished prose cannot rescue the paper.

<Callout type="tip">

**Write for verification.** The reader should be able to reproduce the logic without guessing what a variable means, where data came from, or why a method was selected.

</Callout>

## Argument

Before writing paragraphs, build a claim-evidence outline. For each section, write one sentence completing: “After reading this section, the reader should believe that …” Then list the evidence required.

For a flood-evacuation problem, the outline may be:

1. demand and road capacity create a measurable clearance problem;
2. a time-expanded network represents movement and conservation;
3. the optimization finds feasible departure and routing decisions;
4. simulations under uncertain demand test performance;
5. the recommended policy improves clearance without shifting risk unfairly.

This is different from a chronological diary: downloaded data, tried regression, changed solver, drew plots. Readers need the final logical path, with failed attempts mentioned only when they illuminate a decision or limitation.

Section titles should be short and informative. “Model Development” is acceptable but vague; “Time-Expanded Evacuation Network” tells the reader what mathematical object appears. Avoid sentence-length titles that overload the table of contents.

## Problem

The introduction should establish the decision context, precise objective, scope, and contribution. Restate the problem in your own operational language. Identify decision makers, controllable variables, outcomes, time and spatial boundaries, and evaluation criteria.

Separate facts from interpretation. The prompt may state road capacities and population; your choice to minimize the 95th-percentile clearance time is a modeling decision. Mark it as such.

The problem restatement must not be copied from the prompt. Rewrite it as an operational contract: identify inputs, required outputs, controllable decisions, constraints, and success criteria. This act is part of modeling because it exposes ambiguities before they become equations.

Close the introduction with a roadmap that describes the argument, not headings:

> We first estimate zone-level departures, then formulate a capacitated dynamic network, compare routing policies under matched demand scenarios, and finally identify the conditions under which the recommended schedule loses feasibility.

A literature or background section should support choices. Do not paste summaries of unrelated algorithms. Organize sources by the design questions they answer: evacuation demand, dynamic flow, uncertainty, and validation. State what you adopt and what gap remains.

## Assumptions

Assumptions are controlled approximations, not apologies. Each should include statement, reason, consequence, and test.

Weak:

> We assume traffic is stable.

Stronger:

> Within each five-minute interval, link travel time is treated as constant to keep the time-expanded network linear. We test 10% and 25% congestion multipliers; recommendations that fail those scenarios are not considered robust.

Classify assumptions:

- boundary assumptions define what is outside the system;
- behavioral assumptions describe agents;
- distributional assumptions describe randomness;
- numerical assumptions enable approximation;
- policy assumptions define admissible decisions.

Do not assume the desired conclusion. “We assume our selected sites are optimal” is circular. Do not call observed facts assumptions. Put parameter estimates and data definitions in their proper sections.

<Callout type="tip">

**Assumption audit.** Ask what result would change if the assumption were false, whether the direction of bias is known, and which stress test can expose the failure.

</Callout>

## Notation

Introduce notation close to first use and provide a compact symbol table. Include symbol, meaning, unit, index set, and type. Distinguish data, decision variables, state variables, and outputs typographically or explicitly.

For example:

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit / type |
|---|---|---|
| $x_{ijt}$ | evacuees entering link $(i,j)$ in interval $t$ | people, decision |
| $u_{it}$ | people waiting in zone $i$ | people, state |
| $c_{ij}$ | link capacity per interval | people, data |
| $\tau_{ij}$ | link travel delay | intervals, data |

Then a conservation equation can be read:

$$u_{i,t+1}=u_{it}+a_{it}+\sum_jx_{ji,t-\tau_{ji}}-\sum_jx_{ijt}.$$

Each term has units of people. The equation states that next inventory equals current inventory plus external arrivals and delayed inflow minus departures. Explain this in words immediately after the formula. Equations should compress reasoning, not replace it.

Number equations that will be referenced. Use aligned environments for multi-line formulations, define index ranges, and keep sign conventions consistent. Never use the same symbol for both a set and a scalar.

## Model

Build from a baseline to the final structure. First present the smallest credible model, solve or reason about a tiny example, then add the mechanism that the baseline misses.

For a linear optimization model, present decisions, objective, and constraints as a system:

$$
\begin{aligned}
\min_x\quad &\sum_{t,i}u_{it}\\
\text{s.t.}\quad
&\text{flow conservation},\\
&0\le x_{ijt}\le c_{ij},\\
&u_{it}\ge0.
\end{aligned}
$$

Do not write “Constraint (3) ensures feasibility” and move on. Explain which physical rule it encodes, why its direction is correct, and how its units close. Group constraints by mechanism: conservation, capacity, logic, fairness, and boundary conditions.

If the final model is nonlinear or stochastic, identify where nonlinearity or uncertainty enters. State whether the problem is convex, mixed-integer, or heuristic; this determines what an optimizer's result means. A local solution should not be called globally optimal without supporting theory or bounds.

Include an architecture diagram when multiple models pass information. Arrows should label quantities—demand scenarios, coefficients, decisions—not vague verbs such as “process.”

## Solution

The solution section connects mathematics to computation. Name the algorithm and justify it from model structure. For a convex quadratic program, interior-point or first-order methods have relevant guarantees. For a mixed-integer model, report solver, optimality gap, time limit, and hardware. For a stochastic heuristic, report seeds, population or chain settings, stopping rule, and repeated-run variability.

Pseudocode should communicate the procedure rather than copy library calls:

1. construct demand scenarios from the validated forecast;
2. build time-expanded nodes and feasible arcs;
3. solve the baseline deterministic model;
4. evaluate its policy across all scenarios;
5. update or re-optimize under the chosen risk criterion;
6. record feasibility, objective, and runtime.

Provide one hand-checkable instance. A network with two zones, one shelter, and three periods can verify conservation and capacity. Record an intermediate flow, not just the final objective. That value becomes a unit test for code.

Reproducibility requires data version, code version, random seeds, environment, and important solver settings. A statement such as “we used Python” is not enough.

During implementation, debug from small blocks outward. In a notebook, make each block expose a meaningful intermediate value: cleaned row count, one transition probability, one conservation residual, one objective component. Verify the block before connecting it to the next. A thousand-line script that prints only the final answer makes a simple indexing error almost impossible to locate.

## Evidence

Results should follow questions, not software output order. Begin with the baseline. Then show the proposed method, uncertainty, and decision effect. A good figure caption states the finding:

> The robust schedule clears all zones within 75 minutes in 92% of scenarios, while the nominal schedule fails when downtown demand exceeds the training maximum.

Tables should have units, sample scope, meaningful precision, and highlighted comparisons without visual clutter. Report denominators. A “92% success rate” means little unless the scenario count and definition of success are known.

Separate fit, prediction, and decision metrics. A model can fit historical flow well but recommend an infeasible route. Validation should include:

- data checks and held-out performance;
- mathematical invariants such as conservation;
- numerical convergence or optimality information;
- comparison with simple baselines;
- sensitivity to parameters and assumptions;
- stress tests beyond nominal data;
- decision-level cost, risk, and fairness.

Do not hide negative results. If a method improves mean clearance but worsens the worst district, show the trade-off. Modeling is not a search for a universally superior curve; it is a structured decision under competing consequences.

## Discussion

Interpret mechanisms, not just rankings. Explain why the model behaves as observed. If one bottleneck road determines clearance, identify its shadow price or saturation pattern. If uncertainty changes the chosen shelter, explain which scenario or threshold drives the switch.

Sensitivity should produce thresholds. “The model is sensitive to capacity” is vague. “The recommendation changes only when bridge capacity falls below 1,200 people per hour” supports action.

Distinguish uncertainty sources: measurement, parameter estimation, future randomness, structural omission, and stakeholder preference. A confidence interval addresses only some of them. Discuss transferability: what would need recalibration in another city, season, or population?

Limitations should be specific and connected to consequence. “More data would improve the model” is universal and weak. “Mobile-phone departure estimates underrepresent older residents, which may bias zone demand downward; we therefore retain a 15% reserve in districts with the largest coverage gap” is useful.

## Conclusion

The conclusion closes the argument. Restate the decision, most important quantitative evidence, robustness boundary, and recommendation. Do not introduce a new method or result. Do not simply repeat the abstract word for word.

A useful structure is:

1. one sentence answering the decision;
2. one or two numbers supporting it;
3. the condition under which it changes;
4. the next operational action.

Appendices hold derivations, extended diagnostics, data dictionaries, and supplemental figures that support verification but would interrupt the main argument. The main text must remain understandable without forcing the judge to reconstruct essential logic from an appendix.

## Review

Run four passes:

- **logic pass:** every claim has evidence and every section advances the argument;
- **mathematics pass:** symbols, units, indices, constraints, and calculations are correct;
- **evidence pass:** baselines, uncertainty, sensitivity, and limitations are visible;
- **design pass:** figures, tables, captions, typography, and references are consistent.

Finally perform a cold read. Can someone identify the decision from page one? Can they trace the headline result to a figure and then to a model equation? Can they tell when the recommendation fails? If yes, the paper is not merely complete; it is verifiable.

The main text is successful when the reader never has to trust a mysterious leap. They may disagree with an assumption or preference, but they can locate it, change it, and understand the consequence. That is what scientific modeling writing is for.

欢迎——打开一个空白文档，但不要从引言开始。一篇优秀的建模论文不是团队尝试过程的日记。它是按照读者需要的顺序重建的论证：问题、假设、模型、解法、证据、决定和限制。

核心原则是可追溯性。每个符号都应参考已定义的量；每个方程都应实现已说明的机制；每个图表应回答一个问题；每个结论都应追溯到证据。当这一链条完整时，复杂的数学变得可读；当链条断裂时，即便是优美的文字也无法挽救论文。

<Callout type="tip">

**为验证而写。** 读者应能在不猜测变量含义、数据来源或方法选择原因的情况下复现逻辑。

</Callout>

## 论证

在写段落之前，先建立一个主张-证据大纲。对于每一部分，写一句话完成：“读完本节后，读者应相信……”，然后列出所需证据。

对于洪水疏散问题，大纲可能是：

1. 需求和道路容量造成可测量的清空问题；
2. 时间扩展网络表示移动和守恒；
3. 优化找到可行的出发时间和路线决策；
4. 在不确定需求下的模拟测试性能；
5. 推荐的政策改善了清算，而没有不公平地转移风险。

这不同于按时间顺序的日记：下载数据，尝试回归，更改求解器，绘制图表。读者需要最终的逻辑路径，只有在失败的尝试能说明某个决定或限制时才提及。

章节标题应简短且信息丰富。“模型开发”是可以接受的，但含义模糊；“时间扩展疏散网络”则告诉读者出现了什么数学对象。避免使用冗长的句子作为标题，以免使目录过于繁杂。

## 问题

引言应建立决策背景、明确目标、范围和贡献。用你自己的操作性语言重述问题。识别决策者、可控变量、结果、时空边界和评估标准。

将事实与解释分开。提示可能说明道路容量和人口；你选择最小化第95百分位清除时间是建模决策。标记为此类决策。

问题重述不得直接复制提示内容。将其改写为操作性合同：识别输入、所需输出、可控决策、约束和成功标准。这一过程是建模的一部分，因为它在尚未形成方程前暴露了模糊之处。

在引言的结尾提供一个描述论证的路线图，而不是标题：

> 我们首先估算各区域的出发量，然后建立一个有容量限制的动态网络，在匹配需求场景下比较路由策略，最后确定推荐计划失去可行性的条件。

文献或背景部分应该支持所做的选择。不要粘贴不相关算法的摘要。按设计问题组织来源：撤离需求、动态流、不确定性和验证。说明你采用了什么方法以及剩下的空白。

## 假设

假设是受控的近似，而不是道歉。每个假设都应包括陈述、原因、后果和检验。

弱假设：

> 我们假设交通稳定。

强假设：

> 在每个五分钟时间间隔内，路段旅行时间被视为恒定，以保持时间扩展网络的线性。我们测试10%和25%的拥堵系数；在这些场景下失败的建议不被认为是稳健的。

分类假设：

- 边界假设定义系统之外的内容；
- 行为假设描述代理。
- 分布假设描述随机性；
- 数值假设使近似成为可能；
- 政策假设定义可接受的决策。

不要假设所期望的结论。“我们假设所选择的地点是最佳的”是循环论证。不要将观察到的事实称为假设。将参数估计和数据定义放在它们适当的部分。

<Callout type="tip">

**假设审计。** 询问如果假设是错误的，会产生什么结果，是否已知偏差的方向，以及哪种压力测试可以揭示这种失败。

</Callout>

## 符号表示

在首次使用时引入符号表示，并提供一个简明的符号表。包括符号、含义、单位、索引集和类型。在排版上或通过明确标注区分数据、决策变量、状态变量和输出。

例如：

| 符号 | 意义 | 单位 / 类型 |
|---|---|---|
| $x_{ijt}$ | 进入链接的撤离者 $(i,j)$ 在时间间隔内 $t$ | 人数，决策 |
| $u_{it}$ | 在区域等待的人 $i$ | 人数，状态 |
| $c_{ij}$ | 每个时间间隔的链接容量 | 人数，数据 |
| $\tau_{ij}$ | 链接旅行延迟 | 间隔，数据 |

然后可以写出一个守恒方程：

$$u_{i,t+1}=u_{it}+a_{it}+\sum_jx_{ji,t-\tau_{ji}}-\sum_jx_{ijt}.$$

每一项的单位都是人数。该方程表示，下期库存等于当前库存加上外部到达和延迟流入减去离开。在公式之后立即用文字解释这一点。方程应简化推理，而不是取代推理。

给将被引用的方程编号。多行公式使用对齐环境，定义索引范围，并保持符号约定一致。不要对集合和标量使用相同的符号。

## 模型

从基线构建到最终结构。首先展示最小可信模型，求解或推理一个小示例，然后加入基线所缺失的机制。

对于线性优化模型，将决策、目标和约束以系统形式呈现：

$$
\begin{aligned}
\min_x\quad &\sum_{t,i}u_{it}\\
\text{s.t.}\quad
&\text{flow conservation},\\
&0\le x_{ijt}\le c_{ij},\\
&u_{it}\ge0.
\end{aligned}
$$

不要写“约束(3)确保可行性”然后直接跳过。解释它编码了哪个物理规则，为什么方向正确，以及单位如何闭合。按机制对约束进行分组：守恒、容量、逻辑、公平性和边界条件。

如果最终模型是非线性或随机的，说明非线性或不确定性是从哪里进入的。说明问题是凸问题、混合整数问题还是启发式问题；这决定了优化器结果的含义。没有理论或界限支持，不应称局部解为全局最优。

在多个模型传递信息时，应包含架构图。箭头应标注数量——需求场景、系数、决策——而不是模糊动词如“处理”。

## 解决方案

解决方案部分将数学与计算联系起来。命名算法并从模型结构进行说明。对于凸二次规划，可以使用内点法或一阶方法，它们具有相关保证。对于混合整数模型，需要报告求解器、最优性差距、时间限制和硬件规格。对于随机启发式算法，需要报告随机种子、种群或链的设置、终止规则以及重复运行的变异性。

伪代码应传达流程而非复制库调用：

1. 从已验证的预测中构建需求情景；
2. 构建时间展开节点和可行弧；
3. 求解基线确定性模型；
4. 评估其在所有情景下的策略；
5. 根据选择的风险标准更新或重新优化；
6. 记录可行性、目标值和运行时间。

提供一个可手工检查的实例。具有两个区域、一个避难所和三个时段的网络可以验证守恒性和容量。记录一个中间流量，而不仅仅是最终目标值。该值将成为代码的单元测试。

可重复性需要数据版本、代码版本、随机种子、环境以及重要求解器设置。一句“我们使用了Python”是不够的。

在实现过程中，应从小模块向外调试。在笔记本中，让每个模块展示一个有意义的中间值：清理后的行数、一个转移概率、一个守恒残差、一个目标函数组件。在将模块连接到下一个模块之前先验证该模块。一个只打印最终结果的千行脚本，使得一个简单的索引错误几乎无法找到。

## 证据

结果应该按照问题顺序呈现，而不是软件输出顺序。先展示基线。然后展示提出的方法、不确定性和决策效果。一个好的图注会说明发现：

> 稳健的调度可以在92%的情景中在75分钟内清理所有区域，而名义调度在市中心需求超过训练最大值时会失败。

表格应包含单位、样本范围、有意义的精度，以及在无视觉混乱的情况下突出比较。报告分母。“92%的成功率”意义不大，除非知道情景数量和成功定义。

将拟合、预测和决策指标分开。一个模型可以很好地拟合历史流量，但推荐不可行的路线。验证应包括：

- 数据检查和保留集性能；
- 数学不变量，如守恒；
- 数值收敛或最优性信息；
- 与简单基线的比较；
- 对参数和假设的敏感性；
- 超出名义数据的压力测试；
- 决策层面的成本、风险和公平性。

不要隐藏负面结果。如果某种方法提高了平均通行量，但恶化了最差区域的情况，需要展示这种权衡。建模不是寻找普遍优越的曲线，而是在相互竞争的后果下进行结构化决策。

## 讨论

解释机制，而不仅仅是排名。说明模型为何表现如观察到的那样。如果某条瓶颈道路决定了通行能力，需要识别其影子价格或饱和模式。如果不确定性改变了选择的庇护所，需要说明是哪个场景或阈值导致了转变。

敏感性分析应产生阈值。“模型对容量敏感”过于模糊。“只有当桥梁容量降至每小时1,200人以下时，推荐方案才会改变”则有助于采取行动。

区分不确定性来源：测量、参数估计、未来随机性、结构遗漏以及利益相关者偏好。置信区间只能解决其中部分问题。讨论可迁移性：在另一个城市、季节或人群中需要重新校准的内容是什么？

局限性应具体并与后果相关。“更多数据会改善模型”过于普遍且无力。“移动电话离开估计低估了老年居民，这可能导致区域需求偏低；因此，我们在覆盖缺口最大的区域保留了15%的备用量”是有用的。

## 结论

结论用于结束论证。重申决策、最重要的定量证据、稳健性边界和建议。不要引入新的方法或结果。不要逐字重复摘要。

一个有用的结构是：

1. 用一句话回答决策；
2. 用一到两个数字支持它；
3. 说明其变化的条件；
4. 下一步的操作行动。

附录包含推导、扩展诊断、数据字典和补充图表，这些有助于验证但会打断主论证流程。正文必须保持可理解，无需法官通过附录重建基本逻辑。

## 复习

进行四轮检查：

- **逻辑检查：** 每一个论点都有证据，每一部分都推动论证进展；
- **数学检查：** 符号、单位、指数、约束和计算都是正确的；
- **证据检查：** 基线、不确定性、敏感性和限制条件是清晰可见的；
- **设计检查：** 图表、表格、说明文字、排版和参考文献保持一致。

最后进行冷读。有人能从第一页就识别出决策吗？他们能追踪主要结果到图表，再到模型方程吗？他们能辨别何时推荐会失败吗？如果可以，论文不仅仅是完整的；它是可验证的。

当读者不必相信神秘的跳跃时，正文就是成功的。他们可能不同意某个假设或偏好，但他们可以定位它，更改它，并理解其后果。这就是科学建模写作的目的。
